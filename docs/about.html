Endosymbiotic dinoflagellates in corals

Coral hologenome data are dominated by host sequences, yet the remaining 5 to 10% of reads offer a PCR-free window into Symbiodiniaceae living in hospite. I compiled a scalable workflow that combines assembly-based, alignment-based and alignment-free (k-mer) approaches to assess symbiont communities from hologenomes (Symb-SHIN).

Applying it to 4,013 coral hologenomes from six coral genera across the Great Barrier Reef, Western Australia and the Pacific showed that:

  • Cladocopium dominates almost all coral holobionts, with three major radiations (C1, C3 and C15) and many finer lineages;
  • the environment shapes symbiont communities of mass-spawning corals with horizontal symbiont transmission (Acropora), whereas host genetic identity matters more in brooding corals with vertical transmission (Stylophora);
  • lineage-aware co-assembly recovers symbiont genomes in hospite: 32 Cladocopium genomes from 9 species, revealing the C15-radiation as the earliest-diverging lineage of the genus, with a distinct gene repertoire.

Microbes in coral reef sediments

Reef sediments are a hotspot of nutrient cycling but remain largely unexplored at genome level. From 191 sediment metagenomes collected at 48 sites across the Great Barrier Reef, I recovered 2,698 metagenome-assembled genomes (1,201 species), 98% of which have no species-level representative in reference databases. Compared with co-located seawater, sediment microbes have larger, GC-rich genomes and greater metabolic versatility, including anaerobic respiration, nitrogen fixation, hydrogen metabolism and novel biosynthetic gene clusters (preprint).

At Pelorus Island and Davies Reef, habitat type (front reef, back reef and lagoon) structures prokaryotic communities more than reef location, with metabolically flexible habitat generalists dominating. Sediment viruses, in contrast, are highly novel, local, and structured by reef location.
